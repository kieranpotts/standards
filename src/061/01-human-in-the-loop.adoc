= Human-in-the-loop

AI tools are most effective when directed by experienced software developers. They are much less effective – and, indeed, outright dangerous – when used as autonomous replacements for human decision-making.

**There MUST always be a human-in-the-loop when using AI tools for software development.** This is the golden rule for working with AI tools.

While modern AI models excel at generating code prototypes based on patterns in their training data, production-grade work still requires human skill, experience, and judgment. So too does managing complex projects, inventing novel solutions, and deciding on architectural tradeoffs.

Experienced software developers bring qualities that AI models lack:

* Judgment about long-term maintainability and technical debt tradeoffs.
* Creativity and the ability to make unconventional semantic connections.
* Understanding of how systems interact across multiple domains.
* The wisdom to push back on requirements that don't make sense.
* Context about why certain patterns were chosen in the past.

Like previous waves of programming automation – high-level languages, compilers, debuggers, and IDEs – AI tools automate many tasks, but managing a project's overall scope and implementation still falls to humans.

AIs may be used effectively, with minimal oversight, for rapid iteration of prototypes and experiments. However, for production code, normal engineering workflows must be followed: incremental changes, manual review, comprehensive testing, and proper change management processes.

The normal software development life cycle MUST NOT change when AIs are introduced to automate steps within it. On the contrary, following rigorous development processes, particularly quality control and change management, becomes _more_ important.

Vibe coding is great for anything that doesn't need to be production-grade, but AI-generated code that is destined for production MUST be rigorously validated by a human against the success criteria.

Coding agents are best used to enact human ideas, not as autonomous coding employees. AI tools are most effective when they are used to reduce clerical and repetitive work. The more complex the task, the greater the need for human oversight to ensure quality, maintainability, and alignment with broader project goals.

Setting clear success criteria is key. Well-defined criteria ensure that generated code meets your quality standards and project requirements, and models perform best when outcomes are easy to verify.

Developers who leverage AI tools effectively – by directing them toward well-defined problems and maintaining rigorous oversight – move faster and make better decisions, for instance by using AI to explore more options before committing to one.

AI models tend to follow conventional semantic paths from their training data and rarely make unexpected connections between domains unless explicitly prompted. Human operators unlock novel solutions by guiding the AI toward non-obvious connections.

Early academic research suggests that, the more skills and experience you have as a software developer, the better results you will get from working with AI coding tools. These tools amplify your existing knowledge rather than replace it. You need to remember fewer low-level details, such as the nuances of your programming language. This frees you up to focus on higher-level concerns like design, architecture, and problem-solving – the important stuff, and the stuff that you get better at with experience.

Likewise, the better the quality of your software architecture, the better the results you will get from AI code generators. Without clear tiers and boundaries in your codebase, AI tools are more likely to make sweeping changes that have unintended consequences. Fixing one bug can inadvertently create bugs elsewhere – a time-honored problem in software development that AI tools will amplify if they are not put to work with strong guardrails (enforced at the architectural level) and human oversight (enforced in the software development life cycle).
