= Citations

There are several conventions for including citations and reference lists within documents, such as Harvard, APA, MLA, Chicago, and Vancouver. Most conventions involve the following two parts.

1. A short inline citation within the text

2. A full reference at the end of the text

Prefer the Chicago style of placing an inline superscript number that cross-references a footnote or endnote. In hypermedia formats such as HTML, hyperlink the inline citation to its corresponding footnote, and vice versa, so readers can jump to the footnote and back again.

To reference a work listed in a bibliography section at the end of the document, use the Harvard style for the inline citation. Example:

(Johnson, 2020)

Write the author names exactly as they appear in the reference list.

Use the same formatting convention for references whether they appear in footnotes or bibliographies. The convention is a mix of the Chicago and Harvard styles, and it places emphasis on the author and publication date.

[source,asciidoc]
----
<author> (<year>). _<title>_. <publication>
----

Example:

Johnson, M. (2020). _The Future of Learning_. Horizon Press

When there are two authors, write only their surnames. Example:

Smith and Jones (2020). _Title of the Work_. Publication

When there are three authors, write "Smith, Jones, and Patel". When there are more than three authors, truncate to "Smith et al.".

For essay anthologies, use the editors' names and write "(editor)" or "(editors)" after, eg. "Smith and Jones (editors)".

Omit the year entirely, rather than writing "(n.d.)", when a source doesn't state one. Don't invent a year either. Example:

Johnson, M. _The Future of Learning_. Horizon Press

Render the title in title case.

In hypermedia formats, the title MAY be hyperlinked. When printed, the full URL (including the https:// scheme) SHOULD be appended to the end, after the publication. For an academic journal, include the issue number and page range of the source material. Example:

DeLorme and Fedler (2003). _Journalists' Hostility Toward Public Relations: An Historical Analysis_. Public Relations Review, volume 29, issue 2, pages 99–124. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0363811103000195

Citations of press releases, news stories, and blog posts SHOULD follow the same format. As a general rule, cite the organization or publisher as the author, even if the article has a byline. The following example is a reference to a blog post published on the Cognitect website. The post has a byline, giving the name of its author, Michael Nygard. But the publisher is a more accurate reflection of the authorship.

Cognitect (2011). _Documenting Architecture Decisions_. https://www.cognitect.com/blog/2011/11/15/documenting-architecture-decisions

The following example is a reference to a standards document, cited to the organization that publishes it.

Internet Engineering Task Force (2022). _HTTP Semantics_. https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110

== References sections in the technical standards

The references section at the end of each technical standard is a bibliography, not a plain link list, and it lives in its own `partials/<NNN>/99-references.adoc` partial (see the repository's style guide for the file-layout mechanics). The entry format is a reduced form of the general format, with the publication optional and with a link on the title, and it ends with a period. Each entry is one `*` bullet on a single source line, in this fixed order.

[source,asciidoc]
----
* <author> (<year>). {link-<slug>}[_<title>_].
----

Entries MUST be listed alphabetically by author surname (or by organization name, where the source has no individual author), so that a growing reference list stays scannable rather than reflecting the order sources were added in. Ignore a leading article ("The", "A", "An") when ordering, so "The Economist" sorts under E. Where two entries share the same author, order them by title.

The URL itself is never inlined here. It's declared once as a `:link-<slug>:` attribute and referenced by name. All of a standard's `:link-<slug>:` attributes, whether cited in the references list or only in the body, live together in a single alphabetized-by-slug block at the top of the page (see the repository's style guide for the file-layout mechanics). Where a source has no clear individual author, cite the organization or site as the author, consistent with the press-release and news-story guidance in this topic. An entry cites one work and carries one link. Omit the publisher, unless the name of the site or publication is how readers recognize the source, in which case write it after the linked title. Omit any other annotation. The linked title is enough to identify the source, and a reader who needs more follows the link.
