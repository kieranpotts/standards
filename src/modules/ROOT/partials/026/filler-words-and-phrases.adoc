= Filler words and phrases

Cut a word that adds no meaning to the sentence it sits in — "very," "actually," "basically," "simply," "just," "quite," "really," "so," "in particular," "obviously." Test a candidate by removing it: if the sentence's meaning is unchanged, the word was padding.

Shorten a wordy phrase to its plain equivalent. Write "to," not "in order to"; "because," not "due to the fact that"; "now," not "at this point in time"; "can," not "has the ability to." Delete "it is important to note that" outright, and let the sentence that follows carry its own weight.
