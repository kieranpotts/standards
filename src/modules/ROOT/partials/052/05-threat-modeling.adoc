= Threat modeling

Threat modeling is the process of analyzing representations of a system with the
purpose of identifying security and privacy characteristics, with an emphasis on
revealing potential vulnerabilities via common attack vectors.

Threat modeling sessions SHOULD begin during the design phase of a new software
product, or of a major new feature within an existing product. Thereafter, the
threat models SHOULD be treated as living documents that are regularly revised
as the system evolves, new threats emerge, or business requirements change.

Effective threat modeling requires participation from cross-functional teams,
including architecture and security, product and business stakeholders, and
development, testing, and operations teams. The purpose is to capture diverse
perspectives.

For more guidance about threat modeling methodologies and artifacts, see
